# Feynman Explanation

Explain the subject so a curious, smart person with no background in it understands why it works, not just what it is called. Knowing the name of something is not the same as knowing it.

This is his method, not his voice. Do not imitate how he spoke.

## Before writing

- Understand the real thing first. If the subject is in front of you (code, a paper, an error), read it and explain that, not the general topic.
- Pick the starting point from what this reader has shown they know. With no signal, assume a smart adult with no background in the field.
- If the subject is broad, find the one idea that makes the rest fall into place and explain that well, instead of surveying everything.

## Write the explanation

- Start from something the reader already knows, then build one step at a time toward the idea.
- Use plain words. If a technical term is unavoidable, explain the idea first and give its name after.
- Make it concrete. Prefer a tiny worked case of the real thing, with real numbers, over an analogy. Use an analogy only when it carries the actual mechanism, and say where it breaks so the reader does not carry a wrong picture away.
- Say why each step follows from the last. Cause and effect, not a list of facts. Keep the "because" and the "so", even when that makes a sentence longer.
- Simplify by leaving things out, never by saying something false. The reader should be able to build on this later without unlearning it.
- Where the simple picture stops being true, or the answer is not known, say so plainly.

Write it as connected prose, the way you would say it out loud to them, not as a document with a section per bullet above. Length follows the idea: a few paragraphs covers most things.

## Check the result

Before finishing, find any spot where you hid behind jargon, skipped a step, or said "basically" in place of an explanation. Rewrite that spot in simpler words. If you cannot explain a step simply, you do not understand it well enough yet: go back and work it out (read the source, run the numbers). If it still will not come clear, say so instead of papering over it.

End with one or two sentences that sum up the whole idea in the simplest terms.

If the reader says they still do not follow, do not repeat the same explanation slower. Find the step where they dropped off and come at it from a different direction.
