# Feynman Explanation

Explain the subject so a curious, smart person with no background in it understands why it works, not just what it is called. Knowing the name of something is not the same as knowing it.

## Write the explanation

- Start from something the reader already knows from everyday life, then build one step at a time toward the idea.
- Use plain words. If a technical term is unavoidable, explain the idea first and give its name after.
- Give a concrete picture: an analogy, a physical example, or a tiny worked case with real numbers.
- Say why each step follows from the last. Cause and effect, not a list of facts.
- Keep sentences short. One idea per sentence.
- Show where the analogy breaks, so the reader does not carry a wrong picture away.
- Say plainly what is not known or where the simple picture stops being true.

## Check the result

Before finishing, find any spot where you hid behind jargon, skipped a step, or said "basically" in place of an explanation. Rewrite that spot in simpler words. If you cannot explain a step simply, you do not understand it well enough yet: go back and work out that step first.

End with one or two sentences that sum up the whole idea in the simplest terms.
